Selling through Amazon's fulfilment network changes how packaging needs to work. Your product travels through warehouses, sorting systems and courier vans before a customer sees it. It must arrive intact, pass Amazon's receiving checks, fit within fee tiers that reward smaller packages, and still look good in listing images and when it is opened. This guide covers how to design packaging for Amazon FBA sellers, from protection and prep to listing images and the unboxing.

Amazon's requirements change regularly and vary by marketplace and product category. Use this guide to plan, then check the current requirements in Seller Central before you finalise packaging or send inventory.

## How FBA changes packaging

With Fulfilment by Amazon, you send inventory to Amazon's fulfilment centres. Amazon stores it, picks it, packs it and ships it to customers. That means your packaging must:

1. **Survive inbound shipping** to the fulfilment centre.
2. **Pass receiving checks** and be scannable.
3. **Withstand storage, picking and sorting.**
4. **Ship to the customer,** either in an Amazon box or, for some products, in its own packaging.
5. **Present well** when the customer opens it.

Each stage has implications for design.

## Protection

Products in FBA are handled many times. Packaging should protect against:

- **drops and impacts** during sorting and transit
- **crushing** from other items in totes and boxes
- **moisture** in some environments
- **leaks** from liquids
- **breakage** of glass and fragile items

### Practical protection measures

- **Right-size the box** so the product does not move inside.
- **Use inserts** to hold products and separate components.
- **Choose suitable board:** E-flute or B-flute corrugated for many products; stronger board for heavier items.
- **Seal liquids** with induction seals, tape or caps designed to prevent leaks, and consider bagging them.
- **Protect glass** with inserts and robust outer packaging.
- **Test** with drop tests and real shipments before sending large quantities.

## Amazon prep and labelling requirements

Amazon publishes **packaging and prep requirements** for products sent to its fulfilment centres. They cover topics such as:

- **Barcodes:** each unit must have a scannable barcode. This is usually an Amazon **FNSKU** label or, for eligible products, the manufacturer's barcode such as a UPC or EAN.
- **Poly bags:** requirements for bags, including thickness, transparency and suffocation warnings.
- **Sets and bundles:** items sold together must be packaged so they are not separated, and marked as sets.
- **Liquids, sharp items, fragile items and others:** specific prep requirements.
- **Expiration dates** for some categories.
- **Box content information** for inbound shipments.

Some prep tasks can be done by Amazon or a prep centre for a fee. Designing packaging that meets requirements from the start reduces prep cost and the risk of inventory being refused or delayed.

### Design implications

- **Leave space** for an FNSKU label in a flat, visible area, if you will use one. Make sure it does not cover required information or your retail barcode in a confusing way.
- **Avoid multiple scannable barcodes** that could be scanned by mistake. If you use FNSKU labels, your retail barcode may need to be covered.
- **Mark sets** clearly on packaging when required.

## Size, weight and fees

FBA fulfilment fees are based on **size and weight tiers**. Small differences in packaging dimensions or weight can move a product into a higher tier, which adds cost to every sale.

When designing:

- **Measure the packaged product**, not just the product.
- **Check the tier thresholds** for your marketplace in Seller Central.
- **Reduce unnecessary space** around the product.
- **Consider lighter materials** where protection allows.
- **Compare options:** sometimes a slightly smaller box saves more than it costs to redesign.

Storage fees are also based on volume, so compact packaging saves money over time.

## Ships in own container and Frustration-Free Packaging

Amazon has programmes that encourage packaging that can ship to customers **without an additional Amazon box**, and packaging that is **easy to open and recyclable**, such as Frustration-Free Packaging and Ships in Own Container. Requirements include passing specified tests and meeting material and design criteria. Programmes and incentives change over time, so check Amazon's current certification requirements.

If your packaging will ship without an outer box:

- it must be **robust** enough for courier delivery
- consider **discreet exterior branding** for products that might attract theft
- include **easy opening** features such as tear strips
- **avoid excess plastic** and hard-to-open clamshells

## Packaging that sells in listing images

On Amazon, most customers see your product first as a **small thumbnail** in search results. Packaging plays a big role in the main image and secondary images.

### The main image

Amazon's main image rules typically require a **pure white background** and the product filling most of the frame, with restrictions on added text and graphics. For many products, the packaging appears in the main image. That means:

- the **front of pack** should be clear and recognisable at thumbnail size
- the **product name and key variant** should be readable when zoomed
- **strong colour and contrast** help the product stand out against white

### Secondary images

Secondary images can show:

- the product **in use**
- **key features and benefits** as infographics
- **size and contents**
- **ingredients, nutrition or supplement facts**
- **what is in the box**
- **comparisons** with other products in your range

Consistent packaging design makes these images look like one brand.

## A+ content and brand store

Brand-registered sellers can use **A+ content** to add images, comparison charts and brand stories to product pages, and create a **brand store**. Design these to extend your packaging's colours, typography and imagery, so customers see one consistent brand from search result to doorstep.

## The unboxing on Amazon

Even if your product arrives in an Amazon box, the moment your customer opens your packaging still matters. A well-presented product encourages positive reviews and repeat purchases.

- **Use inserts** to present the product neatly.
- **Include clear instructions** or a QR code to setup videos.
- **Add a short message** or card, staying within Amazon's policies on inserts and review requests.
- **Make returns less likely** with clear sizing information and instructions.

## Packaging and reviews

Packaging problems show up in reviews: "arrived damaged", "leaked", "hard to open", "too much plastic", "smaller than expected". Read reviews for your products and competitors to find packaging issues to fix.

## Selling the same product on Amazon and in retail

Many brands sell the same product on Amazon, on their own website and in shops. One packaging design can usually serve all three, with a few adjustments:

- Keep the **front of pack** strong enough for shelf and thumbnail.
- Use a **robust structure** that survives both courier delivery and shelf handling.
- Plan **barcode placement** so retail scanning and FBA labelling do not conflict.
- Use **outer cases** for retail and **mailers** for D2C where needed.

## Planning checklist

1. **Check current Amazon requirements** for your category and marketplace.
2. **Choose barcodes:** GTIN for listing, FNSKU or eligible manufacturer barcode for fulfilment.
3. **Design for protection** and test with drop tests and real shipments.
4. **Optimise size and weight** for fee tiers.
5. **Plan the front of pack** for thumbnails and main images.
6. **Prepare listing images** and A+ content using consistent design.
7. **Plan inserts and unboxing.**
8. **Confirm prep:** who will apply labels and bags.
