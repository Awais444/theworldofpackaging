Finishes are what turn a printed box into a premium one. They are also where budgets quietly grow. Here is what each common finish does, when it is worth it and which combinations work.

## Foil stamping

A heated die presses metallic or pigment foil onto the surface. Gold, silver, copper, rose gold and holographic are the most common.

- **Best for:** logos, borders and short headlines.
- **Watch out for:** very fine lines and small text, which can fill in. We check minimum line weights during artwork.

## Embossing and debossing

Embossing raises artwork from behind; debossing presses it into the surface. Both can be "blind" (no ink or foil) or combined with foil.

- **Best for:** logos you can feel, especially on textured papers and linen.
- **Tip:** blind debossing on an uncoated paper is one of the most elegant, low-key finishes there is.

## Spot UV

A clear gloss varnish applied only to chosen areas, usually over a matte or soft-touch background. The contrast between matte and gloss makes the artwork catch the light.

- **Best for:** patterns, logos and product photography highlights.
- **Needs:** a separate spot layer in your artwork file.

## Matte, gloss and soft-touch lamination

Lamination is a thin film over the print. It protects against scuffs and sets the sheen.

- **Matte:** calm and modern; fingerprints can show on dark colours.
- **Gloss:** vivid colour, more reflection.
- **Soft-touch:** a velvety feel that customers notice the moment they pick the box up.

## Window patches

A die-cut window with clear film behind it lets shoppers see the product. Great for food, toys and anything whose colour sells it.

## Ribbons and magnets

Not printing finishes, but they shape the experience: ribbon pulls on drawer boxes, ribbon ties on book boxes, and hidden magnets that close a lid with a soft click.

<Note title="Combinations that work">
Soft-touch + gold foil (classic luxury) · Uncoated paper + blind deboss (quiet, tactile) · Matte + spot UV pattern (bold and modern) · Linen wrap + foil + velvet insert (jewellery and watches).
</Note>

## How many finishes is too many?

Most great boxes use one or two. Each finish adds a production step, cost and time. Pick the one that tells your story best and let it lead.
