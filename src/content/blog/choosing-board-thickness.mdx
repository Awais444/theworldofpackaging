Board thickness decides how a box feels in the hand, how well it protects and how much it costs and weighs. Here is how to pick the right one for rigid boxes, folding cartons and mailers.

## Rigid boxes: greyboard thickness

Rigid boxes are built from greyboard, a dense board made largely from recycled fibre, wrapped in printed paper or cloth.

| Thickness | Feels | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| 1.5 mm | Light but firm | Slim boxes, small accessories, lighter products |
| 2.0 mm | Solid, the usual default | Most gift and retail boxes |
| 2.5 mm | Heavy and sturdy | Larger formats and heavier products |
| 3.0 mm | Very solid | Bottles, hardware and premium sets |

<Note title="Rule of thumb">
Start at 2.0 mm. Go thinner for small, light products; go thicker when the box is large or the product is heavy enough that the walls might bow.
</Note>

## Folding cartons: paperboard weight

Folding cartons use paperboard measured in grams per square metre (gsm), typically around 300–400 gsm. Heavier board feels stiffer and protects better, but costs more and is harder to fold on very small boxes. SBS board gives bright white print; kraft gives a natural look.

## Mailers and shipping boxes: flute

Corrugated board is described by its flute:

- **E-flute** is about 1.5 mm, good for mailers and retail boxes that need some crush strength.
- **B-flute** is about 3 mm, better for shipping cartons and heavier products.

## Thickness changes the dimensions

Thicker board takes up more space. Because we work from your product's inside dimensions, the outside of the box grows with the board. If your box must fit inside a shipper or on a shelf, tell us the maximum outside size too.

## Thicker isn't always better

A thicker board adds weight to every shipment and cost to every unit. The goal is the thinnest board that protects the product and feels right for your price point.
