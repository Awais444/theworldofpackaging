A collapsible rigid box looks and feels like a fixed rigid box when it reaches your customer. The difference is how it travels: flat.

## How it works

A fixed rigid box is built as one solid shape. Its walls are glued to the base, so it can only ship assembled.

In a collapsible rigid box, the walls are **hinged** to the base. They fold flat for shipping and storage. To assemble, you lift the walls and they lock upright, usually held by the magnetic lid or by small magnets in the walls. No glue and no tools.

<Note title="In short">
Same greyboard, same wrap, same finishes as a fixed rigid box. Only the construction changes.
</Note>

## Why it matters for freight

Freight is charged on volume as well as weight. An assembled rigid box is mostly air, so a carton fills up long before it gets heavy. Flat boxes stack tightly, so many more fit in each carton.

That means fewer cartons for the same order, which lowers air and sea freight, and less warehouse space once they arrive. You can try the comparison on our [collapsible boxes page](/collapsible-rigid-boxes); the figures there are examples, and we calculate yours with each quote.

## What it costs

A collapsible box takes a little more work to make than a fixed one, so the unit price can be slightly higher. For brands shipping overseas, the freight saving often outweighs it. We quote both options side by side so you can compare the full delivered cost.

## Who it suits

- Brands shipping internationally, especially by air.
- Brands storing stock in a 3PL, where space is charged by volume.
- Seasonal or launch packaging that is stored for months before use.

If your boxes ship short distances and are packed straight away, a fixed rigid box may be simpler.

## Finishes and options

Collapsible boxes take the same finishes as fixed rigid boxes: foil stamping, embossing, spot UV, soft-touch and printed wraps. Inserts are packed separately and dropped in after assembly.
