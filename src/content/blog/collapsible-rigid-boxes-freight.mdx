A fixed rigid box is a sealed volume of air. When you ship five hundred of them across an ocean, you are paying for that air on every pallet.

## Why freight follows volume

Sea freight is charged by volume. Air freight is charged by weight, but light, bulky cargo is charged by **volumetric weight** instead, which is calculated from the carton size. Either way, a box that takes up space costs money to move, whatever it weighs.

A rigid gift box is mostly empty space. Its walls are thin; its inside is the cavity for your product. Shipped assembled, nearly all the carton volume is the inside of the boxes.

## What a collapsible box changes

A collapsible rigid box has its walls hinged into the base during manufacture. Folded, it is roughly as thick as a few layers of board. Unfolded, it locks upright with built-in tape or magnets and looks and feels like a fixed rigid box: same greyboard, same wrap, same foil.

Because the folded unit is thin, many more fit in the same export carton. How many more depends on the box height and board thickness. Our [freight comparison](/shipping) lets you try example figures; for your real numbers, [ask for a quote](/quote) with your size and quantity.

<Note title="Where it matters most">
Tall boxes, large quantities and long routes gain the most. A shallow box shipped domestically gains much less.
</Note>

## Storage is the second saving

The same logic applies after the boxes arrive. A 3PL or warehouse charges for space. Flat boxes take a fraction of the shelf or pallet space of assembled ones, and stay flat until they are needed.

## What it costs you

- **Unit price**: collapsible construction usually costs a little more per box than a fixed rigid box of the same size.
- **Assembly**: someone has to unfold each box. It takes seconds, but plan it into packing.
- **Inserts**: foam inserts ship separately and are placed during assembly.

The freight and storage savings usually outweigh the extra unit cost on international orders. Whether they do for yours depends on the route and the box.

## Is it as strong?

Once assembled, yes for normal use: the walls are the same greyboard thickness as a fixed box. We recommend a [sample](/samples) so you can fold one at your desk and judge it yourself.
