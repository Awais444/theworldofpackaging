Every product brand faces the same question at some point: should we use ready-made packaging, or have it made to our own design? Stock packaging is quick, cheap and available in small quantities. Custom packaging fits the product exactly and makes the brand more distinctive, but involves setup costs and minimum orders. In practice, most brands use a mix, and move along the scale from stock to custom as they grow. This guide compares the options and explains when it makes sense to switch.

## Three levels of packaging

It helps to think of packaging on a scale.

### Stock packaging

Ready-made boxes, mailers, bottles, jars, tins, pouches and bags, available in standard sizes, colours and materials. You buy them from a catalogue, often in small quantities.

**Examples:** plain kraft mailers, stock amber bottles, standard tins, plain stand-up pouches.

### Semi-custom packaging

Stock items or standard structures personalised with your design.

**Examples:** custom labels on stock bottles, branded stickers on plain boxes, printed sleeves around stock boxes, standard box styles such as tuck end cartons or mailers printed with your artwork and made in your size.

### Fully custom packaging

Packaging designed and made specifically for your product.

**Examples:** custom rigid boxes with fitted inserts, custom-shaped cartons, custom bottle moulds, unique structures and opening mechanisms.

## Stock packaging: pros and cons

### Advantages

- **Low minimums:** often available in small quantities.
- **Fast:** ready to ship from stock.
- **Low upfront cost:** no design or tooling for the packaging itself.
- **Flexible:** easy to change suppliers or sizes.
- **Tested:** common stock items are proven designs.

### Disadvantages

- **Generic:** competitors may use the same packaging.
- **Imperfect fit:** standard sizes may be too big, needing void fill and increasing shipping cost.
- **Limited branding:** you are limited to labels, stickers and inserts.
- **Higher unit costs at volume** compared with custom options in some cases.

### Best for

Testing a product, very small volumes, samples, early-stage brands and products where packaging is not a major part of the purchase decision.

## Semi-custom packaging: pros and cons

### Advantages

- **More distinctive** than stock, with your design on every unit.
- **Better fit** when standard structures are made to your size.
- **Moderate minimums** and setup costs.
- **Good balance** of cost, speed and brand impact.

### Disadvantages

- **Some setup costs:** printing plates, dies or label setup.
- **Container shapes** may still be generic.
- **Lead times** longer than pure stock.

### Best for

Most growing brands. Semi-custom packaging is where many brands spend most of their time.

## Fully custom packaging: pros and cons

### Advantages

- **Maximum distinctiveness:** a shape, structure or experience customers associate with you.
- **Perfect fit:** packaging designed around the product, reducing movement, damage and void fill.
- **Premium experience:** custom rigid boxes, inserts and opening mechanisms.
- **Efficiency at scale:** right-sized packaging can reduce shipping and storage costs.
- **Protection** designed for the product's exact needs.

### Disadvantages

- **Higher upfront costs:** design, tooling, moulds and proofing.
- **Higher minimums.**
- **Longer lead times,** especially for custom moulds.
- **Less flexibility** to change quickly.

### Best for

Established products with steady volumes, premium and gift products, products where packaging is central to the brand, and products with specific protection needs.

## Comparing the options

| Factor | Stock | Semi-custom | Fully custom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minimums | Very low | Low to moderate | Moderate to high |
| Upfront cost | Very low | Moderate | Higher |
| Lead time | Short | Medium | Longer |
| Fit | Approximate | Good | Exact |
| Brand impact | Low | Medium to high | Highest |
| Flexibility | High | Medium | Lower |

## The real cost comparison

Comparing unit prices alone can mislead. Consider the **total cost** of packaging a product and getting it to the customer:

- **Packaging unit cost**
- **Setup costs** such as plates, dies and moulds, spread over the order quantity
- **Void fill** needed for oversized stock packaging
- **Shipping costs,** which depend on size and weight
- **Storage costs** for packaging and finished goods
- **Damage and returns** caused by poor fit
- **Packing labour,** which can be higher for stock packaging needing extra protection
- **Sales impact:** packaging that sells better can justify higher costs

Right-sized custom packaging can sometimes cost more per unit but less overall.

## When to move up the scale

Signs it is time to move from stock to semi-custom, or semi-custom to custom:

- **Volumes have grown** enough that minimums are no longer a barrier.
- **Shipping costs are high** because packaging is too big.
- **Products are getting damaged** in transit.
- **Competitors look the same** because they use the same stock packaging.
- **You are moving into retail,** where shelf presence matters more.
- **Your price point has risen,** and packaging needs to match.
- **Customers comment on packaging** in reviews, positively or negatively.

## Sustainability: stock or custom?

Neither option is automatically more sustainable. Stock packaging avoids tooling and can be bought in small quantities, which reduces the risk of waste from unused stock. But oversized stock boxes use more material and need void fill, and they take up more space in shipping. Right-sized custom packaging can cut material, fillers and freight volume significantly, especially at scale. Whichever you choose, look for recycled or certified materials, avoid unnecessary plastic, and order quantities you will actually use.

## Making stock packaging work harder

If you are using stock packaging, you can still make it look branded:

- **Custom labels** with strong design and premium materials.
- **Printed sleeves** or belly bands around stock boxes.
- **Stickers and seals** for closure and branding.
- **Branded tissue paper** and inserts.
- **Stamps** for small runs on kraft boxes.
- **Consistent system** across all items.

## Moving to custom without big risk

To reduce risk when moving to custom packaging:

1. **Start with one hero product** or format.
2. **Use standard structures** printed to your design before investing in custom shapes.
3. **Review a digital proof and 3D mockup** before you approve production.
4. **Order smaller quantities first** if the supplier allows.
5. **Keep a stock fallback** for urgent needs.
6. **Own your artwork and specifications** so you can reorder or change suppliers.

## Examples by stage of growth

**Launch stage:** stock jars or bottles with custom labels, plain kraft mailers with a branded sticker and a printed insert card. Low minimums keep cash free for marketing and stock.

**Growth stage:** printed mailers made to the product's size, printed retail cartons in a standard tuck end style, custom labels on stock containers with a distinctive closure. Packaging starts to build recognition.

**Established stage:** custom rigid gift boxes with fitted inserts for premium lines, shelf-ready cases for retail, right-sized shipping boxes to reduce freight, and possibly custom containers for hero products.

## Questions to ask before switching

1. What volumes do we sell, and what are the supplier's minimums?
2. How much do we spend on void fill, shipping and damage with current packaging?
3. Would custom packaging help us win retail listings or raise prices?
4. Can we afford the setup costs and stock holding?
5. Which product would benefit most from custom packaging first?
6. Do we have the storage space for larger custom orders, or can the supplier deliver in batches?
7. Who will own the artwork, dielines and any tooling if we change suppliers later?
8. How long will this design stay current before we expect to change it?

## Common mistakes

- **Staying on stock packaging too long,** paying for oversized boxes and void fill.
- **Jumping to fully custom too early,** tying up cash in large minimums.
- **Comparing unit prices only.**
- **Ignoring fit and protection.**
- **Not testing custom packaging** before full production.
