The container you choose affects almost everything about your product: how long it lasts, how it feels in the hand, how much it costs to ship, how customers perceive it and what happens to it after use. Glass, plastic and aluminium are the three most common choices for bottles, jars, tubes and cans. Each has strengths and trade-offs. This guide compares them on the factors that matter most to brands, and explains how the choice affects label and packaging design.

The right answer depends on your product, market and priorities. Use this guide to frame the decision, then test with your filler and supplier.

## The factors that matter

When comparing container materials, consider:

1. **Product protection:** barrier against oxygen, moisture and light; chemical compatibility.
2. **Durability:** resistance to breakage and damage.
3. **Weight and shipping:** cost and carbon impact of transport.
4. **Cost:** container price, filling, secondary packaging and breakage.
5. **Brand perception:** how customers see the material.
6. **Sustainability:** recycled content, recyclability and life cycle impact.
7. **Design options:** shapes, colours, decoration and labels.
8. **Practicalities:** filling lines, closures, minimum orders and lead times.

## Glass

### Strengths

- **Excellent barrier:** glass is impermeable to oxygen and moisture, and inert, so it does not react with most products. That makes it a strong choice for products sensitive to oxygen or flavour change.
- **Premium perception:** glass feels heavy and solid. It is associated with quality in spirits, wine, fragrance, skincare and premium food.
- **Clarity:** clear glass shows the product; amber and other tinted glass can protect against light.
- **Recyclability:** glass can be recycled repeatedly without losing quality, and many regions have established collection systems.

### Weaknesses

- **Weight:** glass is heavy, which increases shipping costs and transport emissions.
- **Breakage:** it can break in transit or use, which means more protective secondary packaging and possible safety issues.
- **Energy-intensive production:** glass manufacturing requires high temperatures.

### Best for

Spirits and wine, fragrance, premium skincare, sauces and preserves, products where purity or flavour is critical, and products sold mainly locally or in retail.

## Plastic

"Plastic" covers many different materials, such as **PET, HDPE, PP, LDPE** and others, each with different properties.

### Strengths

- **Light weight:** much lighter than glass, reducing shipping costs.
- **Durability:** shatter-resistant, suitable for bathrooms, travel and children's products.
- **Versatility:** can be moulded into almost any shape, with squeezable, flexible or rigid forms.
- **Cost:** often lower unit cost, especially at volume.
- **Functional closures:** pumps, flip caps and dispensers are widely available.

### Weaknesses

- **Barrier varies:** some plastics allow oxygen or moisture through, which can affect shelf life. Barrier layers can help but may complicate recycling.
- **Perception:** some customers see plastic as cheaper or less sustainable.
- **Recycling:** recyclability depends heavily on the type of plastic, colour and local systems. PET and HDPE are widely collected in many regions; others much less so.
- **Regulation:** many markets are introducing rules on plastic packaging, recycled content and taxes.

### Best for

Personal care such as shampoos and lotions, household products, supplements, beverages for on-the-go use, products needing squeeze or pump dispensing, and e-commerce products where weight and breakage matter.

## Aluminium

Aluminium is used for **cans, bottles, tubes, tins and jars.**

### Strengths

- **Light and strong:** very light for its strength, and resistant to breakage.
- **Excellent barrier:** blocks light, oxygen and moisture completely, which is good for drinks and sensitive products.
- **Recyclability:** aluminium can be recycled repeatedly, and used drinks cans are among the most commonly recycled packaging in many countries. Recycled aluminium uses far less energy than new.
- **Modern perception:** aluminium bottles and tins can look premium and contemporary.
- **Chilling:** cans cool quickly, which suits drinks.

### Weaknesses

- **Opacity:** customers cannot see the product.
- **Dents:** aluminium can dent in transit.
- **Linings:** aluminium containers usually need an internal lining to protect the product and the metal.
- **Minimum orders:** printed cans and custom shapes can have high minimum quantities; shrink sleeves or labels on plain cans are an alternative for small runs.
- **Production energy:** primary aluminium production is energy-intensive, which is why recycled content matters.

### Best for

Beverages, especially carbonated drinks, beer and seltzers; balms and solid products in tins; refillable bottles; and products that need complete light protection.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Factor | Glass | Plastic | Aluminium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barrier | Excellent | Varies by type | Excellent |
| Weight | Heavy | Light | Very light |
| Breakage risk | High | Low | Low (can dent) |
| Visibility of product | Clear or tinted | Clear or opaque | Opaque |
| Premium perception | High | Varies | Medium to high |
| Recyclability | High where collected | Varies widely | High where collected |
| Shipping cost | Higher | Lower | Lower |

## Sustainability: it depends

There is no universal winner. The environmental impact of a container depends on:

- **weight** and **transport distance**
- **recycled content**
- **local collection and recycling rates**
- whether it is **reused or refilled**
- the **product's shelf life**, since wasted product has its own impact

For example, a heavy glass bottle shipped across the world may have a higher transport impact than a lighter alternative, while a glass bottle in a local refill system may perform very well. If sustainability is a key part of your brand, ask suppliers for data and consider a life cycle assessment for major decisions.

## How the material affects label and packaging design

### Glass

- **Clear glass** shows the product, so the label and the contents work together. Clear labels give a "no label" look but need white underprint.
- **Tinted glass** changes how label colours appear around it.
- **Curved shapes** need labels designed for the curve.
- **Direct printing** such as screen printing is an option for premium bottles.
- **Secondary packaging** such as gift boxes and inserts protects glass in shipping.

### Plastic

- **Squeezable bottles** flex, so labels must flex too; film labels often work better than paper.
- **Shrink sleeves** can cover complex shapes with full 360° graphics.
- **In-mould labelling** is possible for some containers.
- **Colour and opacity** of the plastic affect label contrast.

### Aluminium

- **Printed cans** offer full-wrap graphics but have higher minimums.
- **Labels or shrink sleeves** on plain cans suit small runs.
- **Metallic surfaces** affect colours; white base coats or underprint keep colours bright.
- **Tins** can be printed, embossed or labelled.

## Common mistakes when choosing containers

- **Choosing on looks alone,** without testing product stability in the container.
- **Ignoring the filling line.** Some fillers cannot handle certain shapes, sizes or materials.
- **Forgetting secondary packaging.** Glass often needs inserts and stronger boxes, which add cost.
- **Underestimating shipping.** Weight and breakage costs can outweigh a cheaper container.
- **Assuming recyclability.** A container may be technically recyclable but not collected where your customers live.
- **Mismatched closures.** Caps, pumps and droppers must suit both the container and the product.
- **Not planning for regulation.** Plastic taxes, recycled content targets and deposit return schemes are changing the cost of some materials in several markets.

## Making the decision

1. **List your product's needs:** shelf life, sensitivity to oxygen, light and moisture, and dispensing.
2. **Define your channels:** retail, e-commerce or both, and how far products travel.
3. **Set your brand priorities:** premium perception, sustainability, convenience.
4. **Check your filler and suppliers:** compatibility, minimum orders and lead times.
5. **Test:** stability tests with the product, shipping tests and customer feedback.
6. **Plan the full pack:** container, closure, label and secondary packaging together.
