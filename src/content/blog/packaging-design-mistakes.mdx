Most packaging mistakes are not dramatic. They are small decisions that add up: a product name that is slightly too small, a back panel that is slightly too crowded, a box that is slightly too big. Each one makes the packaging a little less effective, a little more expensive or a little more likely to need reprinting. This guide covers the mistakes we see most often, grouped by stage, with a practical fix for each.

## Strategy mistakes

### 1. Designing without a clear brief

**The mistake:** starting design with a vague idea, such as "make it premium", and figuring out the rest along the way.

**Why it matters:** without agreed objectives, every opinion is equally valid, revisions multiply and the result often pleases nobody.

**The fix:** write a brief that covers the product, customer, channels, competitors, positioning, mandatory content, constraints and budget. Agree it before design starts.

### 2. Designing for yourself, not the customer

**The mistake:** choosing colours, styles and messages because the founder or team likes them.

**Why it matters:** your taste is not necessarily your customer's taste, and you see your packaging far more often than they do.

**The fix:** test concepts with real customers, on a shelf or screen, against competitors.

### 3. Copying the category leader

**The mistake:** making packaging that looks like the most successful brand in the category.

**Why it matters:** customers may see your product as an imitation or a cheaper alternative, and it becomes harder to find.

**The fix:** follow enough category conventions that people understand what the product is, then find something distinctive you can own: a colour, shape, illustration style or structure.

### 4. Ignoring the channel

**The mistake:** designing packaging for a shelf when most sales are online, or vice versa.

**Why it matters:** online packaging must survive shipping and work as a thumbnail; retail packaging must stand out at a distance and stack well.

**The fix:** design for your main channel first and check the design in every channel you use.

## Design mistakes

### 5. Trying to say everything on the front

**The mistake:** filling the front panel with claims, badges, icons and benefits.

**Why it matters:** shoppers spend seconds deciding. If everything shouts, nothing stands out.

**The fix:** limit the front to brand, product name, variant, one key benefit and required items such as net quantity. Move the rest to the back and sides.

### 6. Weak hierarchy

**The mistake:** brand, product name and variant all at similar sizes and weights.

**Why it matters:** customers cannot quickly tell what the product is or which variant they are holding.

**The fix:** decide the order of importance and make it obvious through size, weight, colour and position.

### 7. Text that is too small or low contrast

**The mistake:** tiny type, thin fonts, light grey text or text on busy backgrounds.

**Why it matters:** customers cannot read it, older customers especially. Mandatory text may fail legal minimum sizes.

**The fix:** use clear typefaces, adequate sizes and strong contrast. Print at actual size and read it in normal light.

### 8. No system for the range

**The mistake:** designing each product separately as it launches.

**Why it matters:** the range looks inconsistent, customers struggle to navigate it and every new product costs a full design project.

**The fix:** create a system with fixed positions for brand, product and variant, plus a colour or pattern code. New products then drop in.

### 9. Too many finishes

**The mistake:** combining foil, spot UV, embossing, holographic effects and multiple laminates on one box.

**Why it matters:** it can look busy rather than premium, and costs rise with each finish.

**The fix:** choose one or two finishes that support the brand, and use them precisely.

### 10. Designing only on screen

**The mistake:** approving designs from flat files or screens.

**Why it matters:** packaging is three-dimensional and seen in physical light. Screens misrepresent colour, scale and how panels wrap around folds.

**The fix:** review designs as 3D mockups and physical prototypes, at actual size.

## Technical mistakes

### 11. Wrong dieline

**The mistake:** using a generic template or a dieline for a different material.

**Why it matters:** the box may not fold correctly, artwork may misalign and the product may not fit.

**The fix:** use the dieline from your manufacturer for the exact structure, size and board.

### 12. Text and logos on folds

**The mistake:** placing important content across folds, creases or seams.

**Why it matters:** folds crack ink and break text; barcodes across folds will not scan.

**The fix:** keep key content within safe areas, away from folds and edges.

### 13. Colour surprises

**The mistake:** expecting printed colour to match the screen, or the same colour to look identical on different materials.

**Why it matters:** colours shift with substrate, print method and finishes.

**The fix:** use CMYK and spot colour references suited to each material, and approve physical colour proofs.

### 14. Low-resolution images

**The mistake:** using web images or upscaling small files.

**Why it matters:** printed images look blurred or pixelated.

**The fix:** use images at around 300 ppi at final size for most print, or as your printer specifies.

### 15. Barcode problems

**The mistake:** barcodes too small, truncated, in low-contrast colours or without quiet zones.

**Why it matters:** retailers may reject products that do not scan.

**The fix:** follow GS1 guidance, use dark bars on a light background and test printed samples.

## Compliance mistakes

### 16. Mandatory text added last

**The mistake:** designing the front first, then trying to fit ingredients, warnings, nutrition and addresses into the remaining space.

**Why it matters:** text ends up tiny, cluttered or non-compliant, and late changes delay production.

**The fix:** collect all mandatory text before design, and plan its space from the start.

### 17. Unsupported claims

**The mistake:** claims such as "eco-friendly", "natural", "clinically proven" or health benefits without evidence.

**Why it matters:** regulators increasingly act against misleading claims, and customers lose trust.

**The fix:** make specific claims you can support, and have them reviewed by an adviser.

### 18. One label for every market

**The mistake:** using the same label in markets with different requirements.

**Why it matters:** requirements for language, addresses, units and formats differ.

**The fix:** check requirements for each market, and plan market-specific panels or labels where needed.

## Production and cost mistakes

### 19. Boxes that are too big

**The mistake:** choosing a box larger than the product needs.

**Why it matters:** extra material, void fill, shipping and storage cost, and a product that rattles.

**The fix:** measure the product accurately and right-size the box, with a fitted insert where needed.

### 20. Skipping samples

**The mistake:** going straight from digital proof to full production.

**Why it matters:** problems with fit, colour, finishes or assembly appear only after thousands of units are made.

**The fix:** always approve a physical sample for new packaging.

### 21. Ordering too much too soon

**The mistake:** ordering large quantities of a first design to get a lower unit price.

**Why it matters:** cash is tied up and stock may need writing off if the design, regulations or product change.

**The fix:** balance unit price against risk; start with smaller runs where possible.

### 22. Forgetting the full cost

**The mistake:** comparing quotes on unit price only.

**Why it matters:** setup costs, shipping, storage, damage and packing labour can change the real cost significantly.

**The fix:** compare total landed cost, including tooling, freight and storage.

## A quick self-check

Before approving packaging, ask:

- Can a customer tell what it is, and why to buy it, in a few seconds?
- Is it distinctive in its category?
- Does it work on shelf and as a thumbnail?
- Is all text legible at actual size?
- Is mandatory content complete and reviewed?
- Has it been checked on the correct dieline and as a physical sample?
- Is the box the right size for the product and shipping?
- Do you know the total cost?
