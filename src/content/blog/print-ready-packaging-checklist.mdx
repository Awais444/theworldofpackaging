Most delays in packaging production come from the file, not the machine. These are the checks our pre-press team runs on every job. Run them yourself first and your project moves faster.

## 1. Use the right dieline

Artwork must sit on the dieline for the exact box, board and size being made. A dieline drawn for 350 gsm board will not fold correctly on 2 mm greyboard. Ask your manufacturer for theirs, or have one [engineered for you](/packaging-design/structural-dieline).

Keep the dieline on its own layer, set to overprint, and never print it.

## 2. Bleed and safe area

- **Bleed**: extend background colour and images 3 mm beyond every cut line. Rigid wraps need more, because the paper wraps around the board edge and turns in. Check the wrap allowance on your dieline.
- **Safe area**: keep text and logos at least 3 mm inside fold and cut lines.

## 3. Colour mode

Set files to **CMYK** for process print. Spot colours should be named Pantone swatches, not CMYK builds called "Pantone". Remove unused swatches so there is no doubt about what prints.

<Note title="Rich black">
Large black areas print better as a rich black (black plus some cyan). Small text should stay 100% black only, so it stays sharp if the inks are slightly out of register.
</Note>

## 4. Resolution and fonts

- Images at 300 ppi at final size.
- Fonts outlined, or packaged with the file.
- Linked images embedded or supplied.

## 5. Finishes on separate layers

Foil, emboss, deboss and spot UV each need their own layer, in 100% of a named spot colour, aligned exactly with the artwork underneath. Keep fine lines above the minimum thickness for the finish; very thin foil lines can break up.

## 6. Panel orientation

Check which way up each panel will be when the box is assembled. Tuck flaps, lids and bottom panels are easy to get upside down on a flat dieline.

## 7. Text and barcodes

- Proofread every panel, including small print.
- Barcodes in 100% black on a light background, at a size your retailer accepts.
- Regulatory text supplied by you or your compliance adviser.

## 8. Export

Supply a press-quality PDF plus the native file. Name files clearly with box name, version and date.

## What happens next

At our factory, every file is checked against the press it will print on, then proofed. If something fails a check, we tell you what and why before anything is printed.
