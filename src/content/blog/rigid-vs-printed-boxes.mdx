A rigid box and a printed box can carry the same artwork and still feel like two different products. The difference starts with the board.

## What each one is made of

A **printed box** is cut from a single sheet of paperboard or corrugated board, printed, creased and folded. Mailers, tuck end cartons and shipping cartons all belong here. They arrive flat, fold up in seconds and are light.

A **rigid box** starts as greyboard, usually 1.5 to 3.0 mm thick, cut to size and wrapped in a separate printed paper, cloth or leatherette. The board does not flex, which is where the name comes from. Magnetic closure, lid and base, drawer and book style boxes are all rigid.

## How they feel

- Printed boxes flex slightly when squeezed. Good print and finishes can make them look premium, but the hand-feel is lighter.
- Rigid boxes are stiff and heavy for their size. Opening one is slower, and that slowness is part of the experience.

<Note title="Rule of thumb">
If the box is part of what the customer is paying for, it is usually rigid. If it protects and brands the product on its way somewhere, it is usually printed.
</Note>

## Cost and minimums

Rigid boxes cost more per unit: more material, more hand work, more steps. Printed boxes are cheaper per unit and scale down further in price at high volumes. Our rigid boxes start at 500 per design. ## Shipping

Printed boxes ship flat. Fixed rigid boxes ship assembled, which means you pay to move a lot of air. If you need the rigid feel but ship internationally, look at [collapsible rigid boxes](/collapsible-rigid-boxes): the walls fold into the base so they ship flat too.

## Common pairings

Many brands use both:

- A printed mailer outside, a rigid gift box inside, for e-commerce launches.
- Printed cartons for everyday retail SKUs, a rigid box for the gift set.
- A printed sleeve over a plain rigid box, to keep one tooled box across a whole range.

## How to decide

1. Where is the box opened: on a shelf, at a doorstep, or as a gift?
2. What does the product cost? Packaging usually tracks product price.
3. How far does it ship, and how many units per order?
4. Will customers keep the box?
