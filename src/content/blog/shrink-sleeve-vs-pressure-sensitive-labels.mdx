If your product comes in a bottle, can or jar, you will probably choose between two main labelling methods: **pressure-sensitive labels**, which stick on with adhesive, and **shrink sleeves**, which are shrunk around the container with heat. Both are widely used and both can look excellent. They differ in coverage, the shapes they suit, cost, how they are applied and what happens at the recycling stage. This guide compares them so you can choose with confidence.

## Pressure-sensitive labels

### How they work

**Pressure-sensitive labels** (PSL), often called self-adhesive or sticker labels, are printed on a face material with adhesive on the back, supplied on a **backing liner** on a roll or sheet. They are peeled from the liner and applied to the container, either by hand or by a labelling machine. Pressure makes the adhesive bond.

### Strengths

- **Simple application:** can be applied by hand for small runs or by machine at high speed.
- **Flexible materials:** paper, textured papers, white, clear and metallic films.
- **Premium finishes:** foil, embossing, tactile varnish and textured papers are widely available.
- **Lower setup costs:** digital printing makes short runs and many variants affordable.
- **Easy changes:** new variants or updated text can be printed quickly.
- **Shapes:** labels can be die-cut into many shapes.

### Limitations

- **Coverage:** labels cover the label panel, not the whole container.
- **Container shape:** labels work best on flat or gently curved surfaces. They wrinkle on tapered or complex shapes.
- **Liner waste:** the backing liner is waste, although liner recycling schemes exist in some places.
- **Moisture and condensation:** adhesive must be chosen for wet or chilled conditions.

### Best for

Wine, spirits, craft beer, cosmetics, sauces, jars, supplements, candles, short runs, many SKUs and premium paper effects.

## Shrink sleeves

### How they work

A **shrink sleeve** is a printed film tube, usually made from PETG, PVC, OPS or PLA films. The sleeve is cut to length and placed over the container, then passed through a **heat tunnel**, which shrinks the film tightly to the container's shape.

### Strengths

- **360° coverage:** the whole container, from shoulder to base and even over the cap, can be decorated.
- **Complex shapes:** sleeves follow contours, curves and tapers that labels cannot.
- **Tamper evidence:** sleeves can extend over the cap with a perforated band.
- **Durability:** the print is usually on the inside of the film, protected from scuffs and moisture.
- **Shelf impact:** full-body graphics can stand out strongly.
- **Plain containers:** generic stock bottles or cans can look custom.

### Limitations

- **Distortion:** artwork must be **pre-distorted** so it looks correct after shrinking. This needs specialist pre-press and testing.
- **Setup costs:** traditionally printed by gravure or flexo, with higher plate costs and minimums, although digital options exist.
- **Application equipment:** a sleeve applicator and heat tunnel are needed for production volumes.
- **Recyclability:** the sleeve film may differ from the container material and can interfere with recycling if not removed.
- **Fewer tactile finishes:** textured papers and embossing are not available in the same way.

### Best for

Contoured bottles, energy drinks and other beverages, plain cans for short runs, household products, products needing full-body graphics or tamper evidence, and high-volume production.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Factor | Pressure-sensitive labels | Shrink sleeves |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage | Label panel | Up to 360°, shoulder to base |
| Container shapes | Flat or gently curved | Complex contours |
| Application | By hand or machine | Applicator and heat tunnel |
| Setup cost | Lower | Higher |
| Short runs | Very suitable | Possible with digital |
| Premium paper effects | Yes | Limited |
| Scuff resistance | Good with varnish or laminate | Very good (reverse printed) |
| Tamper evidence | With separate seal | Built in with cap band |
| Recycling impact | Usually low, depends on adhesive | Can be higher without perforations |

## Design differences

### Designing pressure-sensitive labels

- Design within the **label panel** and the **visible face** of the container.
- Keep key content away from the **seam** of wrap-around labels.
- Use **white underprint** for clear and metallic films.
- Separate **foil, embossing and varnish** layers.

### Designing shrink sleeves

- Work from the supplier's **sleeve template**, which shows the container's shape and shrink zones.
- Expect **distortion:** areas that shrink more, such as the neck, stretch artwork vertically and compress it horizontally. Your sleeve supplier will apply distortion to the artwork, but avoid placing logos, barcodes and small text in high-shrink areas.
- Place **barcodes** on the flattest area, oriented as advised.
- Plan the **seam**, which runs vertically down the sleeve.
- Plan the **perforations** for tamper bands or easy removal.
- Request **printed test sleeves** on real containers before production.

## Cost: it depends on volume

For **small and medium runs**, especially with many SKUs, pressure-sensitive labels are usually more economical. Digital label printing makes it practical to print small quantities of many designs.

For **large runs** on a single design, shrink sleeves can become cost-effective, particularly when they allow you to use cheaper stock containers instead of printed or custom ones.

Include in your comparison:

- printing setup and plates
- unit cost at your volumes
- application equipment or co-packer costs
- container costs, since sleeves can replace printed containers
- waste and spoilage

## Sustainability and recycling

Both methods affect recycling.

- **Pressure-sensitive labels:** small labels with **wash-off adhesives** or materials compatible with the container's recycling stream have less impact. Large labels on PET bottles can interfere with sorting if they cover much of the bottle.
- **Shrink sleeves:** because sleeves cover much of the container, they can cause sorting machines to misidentify the container. **Perforated tear-off sleeves**, consumer instructions to remove the sleeve, and sleeve films designed to separate in recycling help.

Recycling systems differ between countries. Ask your supplier for materials designed for recyclability in your markets, and make only claims you can support.

## Other options

- **Direct printing** onto glass or aluminium, such as screen printing or digital direct-to-container printing, for premium or high-volume products.
- **In-mould labels** for some plastic containers, applied during moulding.
- **Wrap-around film labels** applied with glue, often used for high-volume beverages.
- **Stretch sleeves** that stretch onto the container without heat.

## How to choose

Choose **pressure-sensitive labels** if:

- your container is flat or gently curved
- you want premium papers, foil or embossing
- you have many SKUs or short runs
- you apply labels by hand or with simple equipment

Choose **shrink sleeves** if:

- your container has complex contours
- you want 360° graphics
- you need tamper evidence built in
- you have high volumes on a single design
- you want plain stock containers to look custom

## Questions to ask your supplier

**For pressure-sensitive labels:**

- Which face materials and adhesives suit my container and conditions?
- What is the minimum order per design, and can several designs share a run?
- Can you supply labels on rolls for my labelling machine, with the right core size and unwind direction?
- Which finishes, such as foil, embossing and tactile varnish, are available?

**For shrink sleeves:**

- Which film do you recommend for my container shape and shrink requirements?
- Will you handle the artwork distortion, and can you supply test sleeves?
- What application equipment is needed, or do you offer application?
- Are perforated or recyclability-friendly sleeve options available?

## Mixing methods

You do not have to choose one method for everything. Some brands use pressure-sensitive labels for small-run or seasonal products and shrink sleeves for their best sellers. Others combine a shrink sleeve with a pressure-sensitive neck label, or use a clear label for a "no-label" look on glass alongside a printed carton.
